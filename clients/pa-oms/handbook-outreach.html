This is not a generic HR handbook. It is PlotArmour's client-acquisition operating system — how we discover commercial opportunities, not just how interns sell. It takes you from a completely new intern to an effective business-development operator, and it tells you exactly who does what and who owns what.

Design, engineer and launch category-defining companies through research, systems thinking and world-class execution.

The mindset you're joining

PlotArmour has two commercial tracks you will represent every day: Agency, which builds digital systems for businesses, and Verity, which builds operating infrastructure for businesses. Both exist for one reason.

CH. 02
Closed client & attribution constitution

What counts as
a closed client

This is one of the most important definitions in this entire book.

DEFINITION — CLOSED CLIENT

A lead is a successful conversion only after it becomes a paying client and PlotArmour receives the advance payment threshold specified in your Internship Agreement for the applicable project.

What does NOT count

  • Lead generation
  • Prospect identification
  • A positive response
  • Conversation, meeting, discovery call
  • Proposal or quotation
  • Negotiation, verbal confirmation
  • "Yes, we want to do it"
  • Intent to proceed

Repeat this distinction to yourself every time a deal looks close. Excitement is not revenue. Only the qualifying advance payment turns a lead into a successful conversion — check your Agreement for how that translates into pay.

Closing edge cases

SituationRuling
Advance received below the qualifying thresholdDoes not trigger a successful conversion. Client stays in Proposal / Negotiation until the threshold in the Agreement is met.
Milestone project (e.g. staged payments against a larger contract)Converts as soon as cumulative payments received clear the qualifying threshold — not on the first milestone alone unless that milestone itself clears it.
Payment reversed, bounced or charged back below the thresholdConversion status reverts to Negotiation. Any pay already counted on that conversion is treated per the Agreement.
Payment attempted but fails outrightClient remains in Proposal / Negotiation — never a successful conversion.
Client pays past the threshold, then later cancelsThe conversion already earned stays earned. It is not retroactively removed.
Payment arrives from a different entity than the one negotiatedValid if traceable to the same commercial relationship and decision-maker. Founders' Office confirms before the conversion is counted.
Client pays after the closing intern has left PlotArmourThe conversion still counts, provided the opportunity was attributed and documented in the CRM before departure. See your Agreement for how it's paid.

Attribution — the roles

Attribution is based on PlotArmour's official internal process — lead tracking, CRM records, outreach records, communication history, timestamps and documented activity. PlotArmour's determination is final for compensation. Three roles exist on any opportunity, and one person can hold more than one:

LEAD ORIGINATOR

The person who first creates a valid, documented prospect record for this company.

OPPORTUNITY OWNER

The person actively progressing the opportunity right now.

CLOSER

The person who directly participates in getting the qualifying advance payment received — this is who the conversion is credited to.

Attribution edge cases

Ownership duration. An Owner keeps the opportunity as long as there is a logged activity — contact attempt, note, or status change — at least once every 14 days. No activity for 14+ days makes it eligible for reassignment by the Senior.
Duplicate leads. The earliest verified CRM timestamp wins origination credit. The later record is merged into it, never deleted.
Pre-existing PlotArmour relationship. Re-discovering an existing client or prospect already in the CRM does not create a new origination credit. It stays with the existing owner.
Inbound leads. Attributed to whoever is on inbound rotation and first responds and qualifies it, per the rotation the Founders' Office sets.
Founder-generated leads. Origination credit sits with the Founders' Office. If handed to an intern to run end to end, that intern gets standard conversion credit as Closer, with no separate origination credit.
Transferred leads. A Senior-approved reassignment gives the new person Owner status going forward. Origination credit stays with whoever discovered it, if it was recorded before the transfer.
Reactivated (dead) leads. Whoever reactivates a lead with a genuinely new trigger becomes the new Owner. Original origination credit is preserved only if reactivation happens within 90 days of the last logged activity.
Cross-team leads. The originating Senior's team keeps it by default. The Founders' Office may reassign for capacity reasons; the two Seniors involved resolve first, escalating only if they can't agree.
Former-intern leads. See the closing edge-case table above — attribution survives departure if the record was documented while the person was active. Payout treatment is per the Agreement.

If multiple people touch the same prospect right now

Do not fight over ownership.
Do not manipulate records, delete another person's activity, or hide previous outreach.
Do not create duplicate records intentionally.
Escalate the conflict and document the overlap.
Allow PlotArmour to determine attribution using the roles and edge cases above.
FIELD RULE

If it isn't recorded, it didn't happen.

Every prospect record should let a Senior Officer or Founder open it and immediately understand who the prospect is, why they were contacted, what problem was identified, what has happened, and what happens next. See Ch. 22 — CRM & pipeline for the exact field list and status states.

CH. 05
Verity

Verity

A separate PlotArmour venture. Do not merge it with Agency.

Configurable business operating infrastructure for businesses with recurring, interconnected workflows.

The problem it solves

Many businesses run on combinations of spreadsheets, WhatsApp, paper processes, disconnected applications, manual approvals, isolated databases, separate accounting tools and fragmented reporting. As they grow, this produces duplication, delays, errors, poor visibility, manual coordination, difficult reporting and operational bottlenecks. Verity brings the relevant workflows into one connected operating environment.

Verity module map

  • Sales — orders, invoicing, receivables
  • Procurement — vendors, purchase orders
  • Inventory — SKU master, stock, warehousing, reorder
  • Production — BOM, planning, shop-floor, consumption
  • Quality — QC templates, inspection, CAPA
  • Projects & Services — jobs, timesheets, work logs
  • Workforce — directory, attendance, leave
  • Finance — expenses, payments, collections, accounting
  • Documents — document management

CH. 08
Qualification doctrine

Market ≠ prospect

Target market ≠ qualified prospect.

A restaurant belongs to our target market. But a random restaurant with no identifiable need, no relevant trigger and no reason to act may not be a strong prospect. A stronger prospect is one opening multiple locations, launching online ordering, struggling with fragmented channels, or growing fast enough to need centralized operations.

GOAL — WRONG

"Find businesses."

GOAL — RIGHT

"Find businesses with a reason to talk to us."

The five-part qualification test

01 · PROBLEM

What is broken, inefficient, outdated, manual, or limiting growth?

02 · TRIGGER

Why might the business act now?

03 · FIT

Does Agency, Verity, or both have a relevant solution?

04 · AUTHORITY

Who makes or influences the decision?

05 · TIMING

Is there a realistic opportunity to act?

A good prospect has Problem + Trigger + Fit + Authority + Timing — not just one of them. Turn this into a number using Ch. 10 — Lead scoring.

Buying signals

Timing creates relevance.

Growth signals

  • Expansion, new locations, new products/services

  • Leadership change, new market, partnership

Pain signals

  • Manual workflows, spreadsheet-heavy operations

CH. 11
Agency vs Verity

Agency vs. Verity
decision tree

THINK AGENCY WHEN
  • Needs a website or new digital presence
  • Needs an app, e-commerce, or a marketplace
  • Wants a custom digital product
  • Needs a customer-facing system or dashboard
  • Needs automation/integration
  • Needs a digital launch, branding + technology, product design

Outreach doctrine

Earn the conversation. Don't send a pitch.

Good outreach is short, specific, relevant, human, respectful and easy to respond to. Show that you researched them, identify an observation, connect it to a possible opportunity, explain why PlotArmour may be relevant, and ask for a conversation.

WEAK

"Hi, we provide website and app development services. Let me know if you are interested."

Generic. No reason to respond. No evidence of research. Focuses on us.

STRONGER

"I noticed you've recently expanded your retail presence. I was looking at how customers currently interact with the business online and saw a few areas that could potentially become a smoother digital experience. Happy to share what I noticed if useful."

Specific, researched, prospect-focused, creates curiosity.

Avoid: "Hello sir, we are a leading technology company offering innovative solutions…" Avoid: "We are experts in cutting-edge AI-powered digital transformation…" Avoid huge lists of services.

CH. 14
Channel playbooks

Channel
playbooks

The outreach doctrine in Ch. 13 is universal. Every channel bends it differently.

LinkedIn

  • Personalize the connection note — never send it blank
  • Wait for acceptance before pitching anything
  • First message references something specific about them
  • Never pitch inside the connection request itself
  • One follow-up after 4–5 days of silence is enough

Email

  • Subject line is specific, not "Partnership opportunity"
  • Opening line proves you researched them, not us
  • One clear call to action — a short call, not a demo
  • Follow-up cadence: Ch. 15
  • Never attach large files or decks unsolicited

WhatsApp

  • Only after some prior contact or a clearly public business number
  • Business hours only
  • Short message — WhatsApp is not email
  • Immediately stop on any opt-out signal
  • Never mass-broadcast the same message

Instagram

  • DM only when the business is clearly run through the account
  • Comment-then-DM reads as more genuine than a cold DM
  • Never send the identical DM to a list — that is spam, not outreach

Phone

  • Open with who you are and why you're calling in one breath
  • Ask permission to continue: "Do you have two minutes?"
  • Qualify before pitching, exactly as in any other channel
  • If a gatekeeper answers, be honest about who you are and ask who handles this
  • Leave a voicemail only if it's specific, never generic
  • Log every call in the CRM — outcome, next action
CH. 15
Follow-up & discovery

Follow-up &
discovery

Follow-up doctrine

Never make every follow-up "just following up." A follow-up should add a new observation, a relevant business development, a useful insight, clarification, or a reference to the earlier discussion.

Default cadence

D0
Initial outreach.
D3
Relevant follow-up — a new angle, not a repeat.
D7
New insight or observation.
D14
Final useful follow-up for this cycle.
D30
Re-engagement, only if something genuinely changed.

When NOT to follow up

Explicit "no" or "not interested"
Unsubscribe or opt-out request, on any channel
"Don't contact me/us again"
Wrong person, and no referral to the right one was offered
Clearly irrelevant business
Abusive or hostile response
A legal or compliance request to stop

What happens after a reply — the decision path

01
Reply is negative or a hard no → classify the objection (Ch. 16) or the rejection reason (Ch. 20), close the loop, do not push further.
02
Reply is positive but no clear problem stated → move to discovery, ask the questions below.
03
A real problem surfaces but PlotArmour isn't relevant to it → disqualify honestly (Ch. 20), don't force a fit.
04
Problem is relevant but the decision-maker isn't in the conversation yet → identify and route to them before proposing anything.
05
Decision-maker is engaged but timing is unclear → nurture on the D30 cadence instead of pushing for a proposal.
06
Problem + authority + timing all confirmed → proceed to full discovery, then Ch. 18 — Proposal.

Discovery questions — Agency

  • What are you currently using?
  • What isn't working? What are customers struggling with?
  • What process is currently manual?
  • What are you planning to launch?
  • What would you change about your current digital experience?
  • Where are customers dropping off?
  • What happens when the business scales?
  • What would a better system enable you to do?

Discovery questions — Verity

  • How do you currently manage customers, leads and quotations?
  • How are orders and invoices handled/tracked?
  • How do you track inventory and manage purchases?
  • How is production tracked? How are approvals handled?
  • How do managers see business performance?
  • How many different tools/spreadsheets are involved?
  • What information is difficult to get quickly? Where does manual coordination happen?
  • What happens when someone leaves? Which process becomes painful as the business grows?
GOOD DISCOVERY LEAVES THE TEAM KNOWING

What the business does, how the process currently works, what is broken and why it matters, what they want to improve, how urgent it is, who is involved, a possible solution, and what happens next.

CH. 16
Objection handling

Objection
handling doctrine

Do not argue with objections. Investigate them.

Every objection below is a request for more information, not a debate to win.

"Not interested."

Ask what prompted that reaction — a bad past experience, no perceived need, or bad timing all need different responses. Don't push past a genuine no.

"We already have someone."

"What's working well with them, and where do you still end up doing things manually?" — opens a comparison, not a confrontation.

"Send details."

A common way to end a conversation politely. Send something specific and short, and ask one question back rather than a generic brochure.

"What's the price?"

Answer with a range only if the scope is understood; otherwise: "It depends on scope — can I ask a couple of questions first?"

"Too expensive."

Ask what it's being compared against. Price objections are usually value or trust objections in disguise.

"Maybe later."

Ask what would need to be true for "later" to become "now." Log the trigger and nurture on the D30 cadence.

"We don't need this."

Test the hypothesis, don't argue it: "Fair — can I ask how [specific process] works today?"

"Talk to my manager."

Ask for a warm introduction rather than trying to circumvent them. Note the referrer as part of the CRM record.

"We're already using Zoho/Tally/Shopify/etc."

See Ch. 17 — Competitor positioning. Never claim to be simply "better."

"Can you do it for ₹20k?"

Never negotiate unauthorized pricing. "I'll check what's realistic for that budget and get back to you" — then escalate.

"What's your portfolio? Who are your clients?"

Share only what's authorized for external sharing. Escalate if a case study is requested that you don't have approved access to.
