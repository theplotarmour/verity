# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-008: Resource Representation Scope

*   **Decision ID**: ADR-008
*   **Question**: Should `Resource` encompass composite spaces, crews, and teams directly, or represent single actors?
*   **Context**: ADR-002 deferred this question deliberately, stating it would be resolved "only after testing human resources, physical assets, rooms/spaces, teams/crews, and capacity pools across scheduling, assignment, availability, conflict detection, delegation, and client composition." That testing has now happened. The foundation validation exercise modelled eight capabilities against the platform (`foundation-validation/capability-analysis.md`) and produced the evidence the deferral was waiting for: five of the eight — Staffing, Facilities, Field Service, Fleet Management and Medical Equipment Maintenance — need to schedule something that is not a single person, and they need it for materially different reasons. Fleet needs a vehicle, Medical Equipment needs a calibrated scanner, Facilities needs a room, Staffing needs a crew, Field Service needs all of them at once.
*   **Affected Concepts**: `Resource`, `Asset`, `Party`, `Scheduling`, `Assignment`
*   **Evidence**: Cal.com models a `Host` as a single bookable actor and expresses a team as a collection of hosts rather than as a distinct bookable type; Odoo's `resource.resource` is likewise a single unit, with `resource.calendar` supplying availability and groupings layered above it. In both, the composite is assembled from singles rather than modelled alongside them.
*   **Evidence Location**: Cal.com EventType/Host schema; Odoo `resource.resource` and `resource.calendar`.
*   **Options**:
    *   *Option A*: Crew availability is modelled directly as a first-class Crew Resource, sitting alongside human and asset resources as a distinct kind.
    *   *Option B*: `Resource` strictly represents a single schedulable unit — a person or a physical asset — and crews, pools, rooms-as-sets and capacity groups are composite structures assembled from Resources.
*   **Recommendation**: Option B.
*   **Status**: `ACCEPTED`
*   **Owner**: Product Owner
*   **Resolution**: Adopt Option B.

    The demand the eight capabilities actually expressed was not for *a different kind of thing*. It was for **something schedulable that is not a person**, which Option B already answers: a `Resource` is a single schedulable unit and may be backed by a `Party` (a human) or by an `Asset` (a vehicle, a scanner, a bay). Crews, pools and teams are then compositions of Resources, not a parallel type.

    1.  **`Resource` is a single schedulable unit.** It carries availability, capacity of one, and exactly one backing identity — either a `Party` or an `Asset`, never both and never neither.
    2.  **Composites are groups, not resources.** A crew, a pool, a shift team or a room set is a `ResourceGroup`: an ordered or unordered collection of Resources with its own selection policy (all-of, any-of, n-of). Scheduling against a group resolves to scheduling against its members.
    3.  **Availability and conflict detection operate on Resources only.** A group has no calendar of its own; its availability is derived from its members. This keeps one conflict-detection implementation rather than two that must agree.
    4.  **Delegation and assignment target Resources.** Assigning work to a group is resolved to member Resources at assignment time, so every assignment has an unambiguous responsible unit and the audit trail names it.
    5.  **Capacity above one is a property of a group, not of a Resource.** A bay that can hold three vehicles is a group of three Resources, not one Resource with capacity three. This avoids a second capacity model inside the scheduler.

    Option A was rejected because a first-class Crew type forces every downstream concern — availability, conflict detection, assignment, delegation, audit, and client composition — to branch on whether it is looking at a single or a composite. That branch would appear in seven places and would have to stay consistent in all of them. Option B removes the branch by making the composite a collection of the thing that already works.

    This decision does not create `Resource`, `Asset` or `Scheduling`. It fixes their shape so those capabilities can be built without re-litigating it.
*   **Supersedes**: ADR-002 (`DECISION_REQUIRED`), DEC-BIBLE-002
*   **Superseded By**: None
*   **Confidence**: `HIGH`

---

## Consequences

*   `Resource` requires exactly one backing reference — a `Party` or an `Asset` — enforced as a constraint rather than by convention, since a Resource backed by neither is unschedulable and one backed by both is ambiguous.
*   `ResourceGroup` and its membership become part of the Scheduling capability's contract, not the platform core.
*   The scheduler implements availability and conflict detection once, against Resources.
*   A capability wanting a "team" does not extend the platform; it composes a group.
*   ADR-002 is closed. `verity-spec/17_decisions/unresolved.md` no longer carries an open Resource question.
