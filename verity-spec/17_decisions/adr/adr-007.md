# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-007: Party De-duplication and Cross-Tenant Identity Linking

*   **Decision ID**: ADR-007
*   **Question**: How does the platform guarantee exactly one `Party` per human or corporate participant (INV-003) when a tenant cannot see identities it holds no membership for?
*   **Context**: `Party` and `User` are global tables scoped to the platform database and mapped to Organizations through `TenantMembership` records (Bible V2, Primitive 2, Section 2). Because they carry no tenant column, tenant isolation for them is enforced by *reachability*: a tenant may read an identity only when that identity holds a membership in it. That is required by INV-001, but it produces a gap. A tenant provisioning a new person cannot tell whether a `Party` for the same human already exists in another tenant, so two tenants can each create one. INV-003 then fails silently, and the subcontractor case in PLA-IDE-004 — one login, memberships in several tenants — becomes unrepresentable. The naive repair, allowing a global lookup before provisioning, converts the identity table into a cross-tenant enumeration oracle: a tenant could confirm whether any given email or phone belongs to a person on the platform, which is a disclosure of one tenant's workforce to another.
*   **Affected Concepts**: `Party`, `User`, `TenantMembership`, `Organization`
*   **Evidence**: Keycloak models a single global user realm and resolves membership through group bindings rather than duplicating the user per realm; Odoo's `res.partner` is a single global table with an `active` flag and no tenant partition.
*   **Evidence Location**: Keycloak UserModel and group-scoped membership bindings; Odoo `res.partner`.
*   **Options**:
    *   *Option A*: Global uniqueness enforced at provisioning. A unique constraint on contact details, and a lookup before insert that links to the existing `Party` when one matches. Guarantees INV-003 directly and is simple to implement, but it discloses existence across tenants on every attempt, and the disclosure is unavoidable because the linking behaviour itself is observable. It also treats an unverified, tenant-supplied email as a global identity key.
    *   *Option B*: Invitation and verification. Provisioning creates a `Party` in the `Invited` state holding unverified contact details, which are local and carry no global claim. Identity is established only when the invitee verifies a contact channel. At verification the platform links: if a `Party` already exists for that verified contact, the new membership is re-pointed at it and the provisional record is discarded. Uniqueness is enforced only over verified contacts. No cross-tenant read occurs at provisioning, so nothing is disclosed until the person themselves acts.
    *   *Option C*: Accept duplicates and reconcile later through an administrative merge. Defers the problem, leaves INV-003 violated in the interim, and makes every downstream reference ambiguous.
*   **Recommendation**: Option B.
*   **Status**: `ACCEPTED`
*   **Owner**: Product Owner
*   **Resolution**: Adopt Option B. The platform distinguishes an **unverified provisional identity** from a **verified canonical identity**, and only the latter is unique platform-wide.

    1.  **Provisioning** creates a `Party` in the `Invited` state with tenant-supplied, unverified contact details, together with its `User` and first `TenantMembership`. No cross-tenant lookup is performed and no existence is disclosed. Duplicate unverified records are permitted and are expected to be transient.
    2.  **Verification** of an email or phone through the evidence mechanism already required by Bible V2, Primitive 2, Section 8 is the sole act that establishes a canonical identity. Verification is performed by the person, never asserted by a tenant.
    3.  **Linking** occurs at verification. If a `Party` already holds the same verified contact, the memberships of the provisional record are transferred to the existing `Party`, the provisional `Party` and its `User` are discarded, and the `accept_invite` transition moves the surviving record to `Active` (Bible V2, Primitive 2, Sections 3 and 5). One human therefore converges on one `Party`, satisfying INV-003 at the point identity becomes real.
    4.  **Uniqueness** is enforced by the database over verified contacts only. Unverified contact values carry no uniqueness constraint, because a tenant must not be able to reserve or probe an identifier it has not proven.
    5.  **Disclosure** is bounded by design. A tenant learns that its invitee already exists on the platform only after that person verifies and accepts, which is an action by the person and not by either tenant. A tenant never learns which other tenants an identity belongs to, and reachability continues to govern all reads.

    This decision does not, on its own, permit a merge of two already-verified `Party` records. That is a separate administrative operation with its own audit and authorization requirements and is out of scope here.
*   **Supersedes**: None
*   **Superseded By**: None
*   **Confidence**: `HIGH`

---

## Consequences

*   `Party` requires verification state for its contact channels; an unverified value must never be treated as an identity key.
*   The uniqueness constraint is partial, covering verified contacts only.
*   `provisionIdentity` remains correct as written and continues not to de-duplicate. De-duplication belongs to the verification path, not to provisioning.
*   The invitation and verification flow is a prerequisite for exposing any identity-creating API to tenants. Until it exists, identity provisioning stays an internal platform operation.
*   Archival remains the terminal state for a `Party` (Bible V2, Primitive 2, Section 3). Linking discards only provisional, never-verified records, so no verified history is destroyed.
