# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-011: Glass as a Controlled Material System

*   **Decision ID**: ADR-011
*   **Question**: Is translucency permitted on persistent surfaces of the Verity Experience System, and if so under what constraints?
*   **Context**: Bible V4 §1.B `[FACT]` restricts glassmorphism. Its text says Verity "must NOT use glassmorphic blur (`.verity-glass`, translucent backdrops) as its **default visual identity**", gives the rationale that "**ornamental** transparency reduces legibility and degrades performance", and lists three allowed exceptions — all of them *temporary* contextual layers (a mobile sheet, a floating cart panel, a dropdown).

    During the experience-shell build that clause was read as a blanket prohibition on translucency anywhere persistent. Acting on that reading, the shell composited every translucent layer in the approved design boards down to its solid equivalent, and recorded the result as settled in `src/app/globals.css`, `implementation/experience-shell-audit.md` and `implementation/experience-shell-visual-gap-audit.md`.

    The product owner has since supplied light and dark reference boards showing an Experience System whose depth and hierarchy are produced *by* a controlled glass material — atmospheric background, layered spatial surfaces, restrained translucency — with the Warm Sand Gold identity intact. The shipped shell does not match them: it reads as a conventional enterprise dashboard of opaque cards and hairline borders.

    The conflict is therefore between the reference boards and an **interpretation**, not between the boards and §1.B's text. §1.B bans glass as a *default identity* and condemns *ornamental* transparency. It does not say that no persistent surface may ever be translucent, and it does not address a material system that is applied selectively to establish hierarchy. The stricter reading was an inference, and it was mine.
*   **Affected Concepts**: Experience System; configurable accent; design tokens; Experience Shell chrome; card, table, control and overlay surfaces; light/dark theming; accessibility contrast; rendering performance
*   **Evidence**: Four observations constrain the answer.

    First, §1.B's own wording is scoped. "Default visual identity" and "ornamental" are both qualifiers. A clause banning a *default* is not a clause banning a *considered exception*, and the rationale it offers — legibility and performance — is a constraint on *how* translucency may be used, not a proof that it never may be.

    Second, §1.B's stated rationale is preserved rather than contradicted by the new decision. Both concerns become explicit, testable constraints below: text contrast is verified against the composited result, and blur layers are capped. A rule whose reasons are honoured is amended, not overturned.

    Third, the prior interpretation is already recorded as a *resolution of a conflict*, not as a reading of plain text. `globals.css` states that the boards' `backdrop-filter: blur(30px)` chrome "is persistent chrome, so §1.B forbids it and the Bible outranks the board". That is an inference about scope, made in the absence of a decision — precisely the class of question an ADR exists to settle.

    Fourth, Bible V4 §1.A is unaffected and continues to govern. "Whitespace as Structure" — hierarchy from alignment and negative space rather than borders, boxes and grids — is the clause the current flat implementation actually violates, by separating information with hairline rectangles. A material system that produces depth without borders serves §1.A better than the solid-card composition it replaces.
*   **Evidence Location**: `verity-bible/volume_4_experience_ux.md` §1.A–1.B; `verity-bible/volume_6_registries_transformation.md` line 137; `src/app/globals.css`; `implementation/experience-shell-audit.md`; `implementation/experience-shell-visual-gap-audit.md`; product-owner reference boards (light and dark)
*   **Options**:
    *   *Option A*: Keep the blanket interpretation. Persistent surfaces remain solid; the reference boards are treated as unreachable.
    *   *Option B*: Permit translucency as a **controlled material system** — applied deliberately by level, constrained by contrast and performance budgets, and never decorative.
    *   *Option C*: Remove the restriction entirely and allow translucency anywhere.
*   **Recommendation**: Option B.
*   **Status**: `ACCEPTED`
*   **Owner**: Product Owner
*   **Resolution**: Adopt Option B.

    Glass is a **first-class material of the Verity Experience System**, used where it establishes depth, hierarchy and focus. It is a material system, not a decoration, and the distinction is load-bearing rather than rhetorical.

    **The canonical principle.** Verity combines Apple-grade restraint and information clarity with a controlled glass material system. The interface should feel light, spatial, premium and calm. Glass creates depth and hierarchy; it does not replace hierarchy, it does not replace contrast, and it does not become decoration.

    **Material hierarchy.** Translucency is assigned by level, and the levels are distinct in purpose rather than in degree:

    | Level | Surface | Treatment |
    |---|---|---|
    | 0 | Atmospheric background | Tonal field, subtle luminance variation, no obvious gradient |
    | 1 | Application shell / navigation | Quiet translucency, integrated with the background, no heavy divider |
    | 2 | Primary surfaces (cards, panels) | Controlled glass: translucency, blur, hairline border, ambient shadow, faint inner highlight |
    | 3 | Secondary translucent surfaces | Weaker glass, subordinate to level 2 |
    | 4 | Interactive controls | Glass-family material, restrained; focus and hover legible |
    | 5 | High-contrast / semantic surfaces | Solid. Status, destructive confirmation and dense content live here |

    **Where glass does not go.** Dense table content, long-form text, high-density forms, semantic status controls, destructive confirmation and accessibility-critical surfaces stay solid or near-solid. Not every card, row, badge, button, icon or divider becomes frosted; indiscriminate glass is the failure mode this decision names explicitly.

    **Binding constraints.** Glass is permitted only while all of these hold:

    1.  **Contrast.** Text over a translucent surface meets WCAG AA against the *composited* result, not against the token in isolation. Where glass would reduce contrast below threshold, the material changes — never the requirement.
    2.  **Performance.** Blur layers are limited and composited. Stacked independent `backdrop-filter` layers are not permitted; the effect degrades gracefully on low-powered devices.
    3.  **Hierarchy.** Glass expresses an existing information hierarchy. A surface does not become glass to look better than its neighbours.
    4.  **Parity.** Light and dark are two material interpretations of one system, not two designs. The same hierarchy survives both.
    5.  **User preference.** `prefers-reduced-transparency` and `prefers-reduced-motion` are honoured.
    6.  **Subordination.** Content clarity outranks material effect in every conflict.

    **Identity and accent are two systems, not one.**

    | | |
    |---|---|
    | **Brand** | The Verity mark and wordmark. Fixed assets. Never recoloured by the interface. |
    | **Accent** | An Experience System property. Configurable. Defaults to Warm Sand Gold. |

    The approved design source is a themeable prototype: it names **Warm Sand Gold `#D4A017`** as its default and offers nine further presets — Champagne, Verity Mint, Ocean Blue, Slate Blue, Indigo, Violet, Emerald, Rose, Graphite — plus custom values. The reference boards render in Verity Mint, which is why they are not evidence that gold was wrong; they are evidence that the accent is a choice.

    So gold is the default because it is the brand's colour, and a tenant selecting Ocean Blue changes the interface without changing the identity. Recolouring the mark to match a selected accent would collapse the two systems and is prohibited.

    Semantic colours — success, warning, danger, info, neutral — remain independent of both: accent carries brand, semantic carries meaning, and neither is forced into the other. A distribution chart may use the accent's tonal ladder, because a distribution is quantitative; a single record's state may not, because state is semantic.

    **Accent contrast is computed, never assumed.** The fill and its ink are chosen together against each other, and the tonal ladder is walked — darker in light, lighter in dark — until the pair clears WCAG AA. Three of the ten presets require that step and Emerald requires two: at its first step neither black nor white reaches 4.5:1. Assuming white works on every accent is the specific failure this rule exists to prevent, and constraint 1 applies to the accent exactly as it applies to glass.

    **What this does not license.** Excessive transparency, indiscriminate blur, decorative noise, low-contrast text, neon or cyberpunk treatment, heavy gradients, excessive shadows, neumorphism, or visual effect substituting for information hierarchy. This decision permits a material system; it does not permit an aesthetic exercise.

    **Relationship to Bible V4 §1.B.** §1.B's *text* — no glass as default identity, no ornamental transparency — is unchanged and continues to bind. What is superseded is the stricter **interpretation** that no persistent surface may be translucent, which is not required by the text and which is what produced the current flat implementation. §1.B's rationale, legibility and performance, is carried forward as constraints 1 and 2 above, where it is now testable rather than merely asserted.

    Bible V6 line 137's "absolute ban on decorative glassmorphism" remains correct and is reinforced: this decision bans decorative glass in the same breath as permitting structural glass.

    `verity-bible/` is **not** edited by this ADR. The Bible's one-time amendment allowance (AMD-001) is spent, and the authority order places an explicit decision above it — so an ADR is the correct instrument for a change of this class, and the constitutional text stays intact and auditable.

    Option A was rejected because it holds an inference above an explicit product-owner decision, and because the flat composition it produces conflicts with §1.A's whitespace-as-structure clause. Option C was rejected because it discards §1.B's rationale: unconstrained translucency is exactly the legibility and performance failure the Bible identified, and removing the restriction would license the decoration this decision forbids.
*   **Supersedes**: The interpretation of Bible V4 §1.B recorded in `src/app/globals.css`, `implementation/experience-shell-audit.md` §"Solid chrome instead of the board's blur", and `implementation/experience-shell-visual-gap-audit.md`. It does not supersede §1.B's text, ADR-009, or any platform decision.
*   **Superseded By**: None
*   **Confidence**: `HIGH`
