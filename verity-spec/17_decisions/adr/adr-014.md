# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-014: DEC-001 Scope — Core Module versus Capability-Private Screen

*   **Decision ID**: ADR-014
*   **Question**: Does Bible V6 `DEC-001`, which permanently excludes a Kitchen Display System from Verity, forbid a purpose-built kitchen screen inside one client's capability?
*   **Context**: Kent's Restaurant requirement #2 is *"Kitchen cook tab/phone — view incoming orders and what to cook."* Every contract that screen needs already exists and passes tests: `verity.dinein.order_line` states with honest categories, the `advance_order_line` command, the `kitchen_queue` query, and `urgencyFor()` from the SLA substrate. Nothing technical is missing — an eighty-line page would complete it.

    `DEC-001 [FACT, ACCEPTED]` reads:

    > **Decision:** The kitchen display system (KDS) and cooking queue tracking are permanently excluded from the Verity **core product scope**.
    >
    > **Rationale:** Verity is optimized for service-driven organizations. Food preparation tracking requires micro-level inventory recipes and highly specific KDS bump timers that do not generalize into other service operations.

    `KentsRestaurant.md` §2.2 raised the conflict rather than resolving it, and classified it as *conflicting specification / missing ADR*. The screen has not been built.
*   **Affected Concepts**: Bible V6 decision register; capability boundaries; PLATFORM-FREEZE's purpose-built encouragement; `verity.capability.dinein`
*   **Evidence**:

    First, **the decision's own words are scoped**. "Core product scope" is a qualifier, and a clause excluding something from the core is not the same clause as one excluding it from everything built on the core. Reading the qualifier out of the sentence would be an inference, not a reading.

    Second, **the rationale is about generalisation**. It names two things — recipe micro-inventory and KDS bump timers — and gives one reason: they *"do not generalize into other service operations"*. A screen that lives inside one capability, consumes only that capability's own entities, and cannot be reused by any other capability generalises nothing. The rationale's concern does not reach it.

    Third, **PLATFORM-FREEZE explicitly encourages this shape**: *"A capability may own real typed columns, its own tables, hard-coded domain rules, a bespoke screen and specialised performance work."* The same document forbids *"a generic scheduler"*, *"more abstract workflow machinery"* and hypothetical modules — the category DEC-001 is defending.

    Fourth, **the alternative is worse for the property DEC-001 protects**. Refusing the screen does not stop a restaurant needing one; it moves the cooking queue to paper or to a third-party system whose state Verity never sees. The order lines would then advance by someone re-keying them, or not at all, and the audit trail — the thing the platform is actually for — would have a hole in it exactly where the kitchen is.
*   **Evidence Location**: `verity-bible/volume_6_registries_transformation.md` §4 `DEC-001`; `implementation/PLATFORM-FREEZE.md`; `KentsRestaurant.md` §1 requirement 2, §2.2, §6 M4, §17 D11; `implementation/decisions-d11-d7-d2.md`; `src/server/capabilities/dinein/index.ts`
*   **Options**:
    *   *Option A*: `DEC-001` governs the core product only. A capability-private kitchen screen is permitted, built inside the capability on public contracts.
    *   *Option B*: `DEC-001` governs any Verity surface. No kitchen screen exists; the requirement is refused.
    *   *Option C*: Amend `DEC-001` to permit kitchen surfaces generally.
*   **Recommendation**: Option A.
*   **Status**: `ACCEPTED`
*   **Owner**: Product Owner
*   **Resolution**: Adopt Option A, with the boundary stated rather than left to inference.

    **`DEC-001` continues to govern the core product, unqualified.** Verity ships no Kitchen Display System, no cooking-queue module, no recipe micro-inventory, no bump-timer machinery, and no generalisable kitchen vocabulary in `src/server/platform/` or in any shared capability. That exclusion is permanent and this ADR does not narrow it.

    **A client capability may build a screen over its own entities.** The Kent's kitchen surface is therefore permitted, subject to all four of the following. They are the boundary; a screen that fails any of them is the module `DEC-001` excludes.

    1. **Capability-private.** It lives in `src/app/(shell)/kitchen/`, reads `verity.dinein.*` entities through registered queries, and mutates through registered commands. No other capability can reach it or reuse it.
    2. **No platform vocabulary.** Nothing named for a kitchen enters `src/server/platform/`. No `KitchenTicket` primitive, no bump-timer contract, no prep-queue abstraction.
    3. **No recipe or inventory logic.** The two things `DEC-001`'s rationale names stay excluded outright. The screen shows lines that were ordered; it does not know what a dish is made of, and nothing depletes.
    4. **Timing rides the existing SLA substrate.** Urgency comes from `urgencyFor()` over the platform's clocks, driven by `StateCategory`. A bespoke timer would be the bump timer `DEC-001` names.

    **Precedent, stated deliberately.** This is the first time a capability builds a surface the core excludes, and the reasoning generalises to the next one: an exclusion aimed at a *generalisable module* does not bar *purpose-built code inside one capability*, provided that code cannot escape the capability. Anything that would escape — a shared component, a platform contract, a reusable abstraction — is the excluded thing wearing a different name, and needs its own decision rather than this one.

    **Why record the boundary rather than simply permitting it.** An unqualified reading of `DEC-001` would be violated the first time any client needed a queue view, quietly and by someone who never read the register. A stated boundary can be checked — including by a conformance test — which is the difference between a rule and a hope.
